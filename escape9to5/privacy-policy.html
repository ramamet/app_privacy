Escape9to5 Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 July 2026

Escape9to5 is an offline calculator for people deciding whether they can afford to leave a salaried job. You type in real numbers: what you have saved, what you earn, what you spend each month. That is private information, and the app is built so that none of it leaves your phone. This policy explains what that means in practice.

The short version

  • Escape9to5 does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data.
  • There is no account and no sign-up.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, and no tracking of any kind.
  • Your savings, income, and expense figures stay on your device.
  • The app makes no network calls of its own. Its only network use is Google Play handling the optional purchase.

The financial numbers you enter

This is the part that matters most, so it comes first. Everything you type into the runway calculator, the tax calculator, the decision score, and the idea store is written to a database inside Escape9to5's private storage on your device. That includes your savings, your monthly income and expenses, how many people depend on you, your city, and every calculation you have saved.

None of it is uploaded. There is no server to upload it to. It is not backed up to any account we control, and we cannot read it, because we never receive it. It leaves your device only if you back up your phone yourself using your own tools. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

What the app does not collect

Escape9to5 does not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, location, photos, or device identifiers. It does not track how you use the app. There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, and no advertising library in the build. The app's own code makes no network requests at all.

The optional one-time purchase

Escape9to5 is free to download, and the runway calculator and decision score work in full without paying. There is one optional in-app purchase that unlocks the rest. If you buy it, the transaction is handled entirely by Google Play. Escape9to5 never sees your card details, your billing address, or your Google account. The app stores a single private flag on your device recording that the purchase was made. Google's handling of the payment is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

To confirm a purchase is genuine, the app checks the signature Google attaches to the receipt. That check runs on your device and sends nothing anywhere.

Why the app asks for internet permission

If you inspect the app's permissions you will see INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE. These are declared by Google's billing library, which the app includes so the optional purchase can work. Escape9to5's own code never opens a connection. Every calculator, the city cost data, and the learning content all work with the phone in airplane mode.

Where the built-in data comes from

The city living costs, startup registration costs, tax slabs, and funding records shipped with the app are static reference data compiled before release and stored in the app itself. Looking any of it up sends no request and reveals nothing about what you searched for.

Deleting your data

You can clear saved calculations and ideas inside the app. Uninstalling Escape9to5 removes the entire database along with it. Because nothing was ever sent anywhere, there is no copy left behind and no deletion request to file.

Children

Escape9to5 is meant for working adults thinking about their careers. It does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children, because it does not collect data at all.

This app is not financial advice

Escape9to5 is a calculator and a set of reference tables. It is not a financial adviser, an accountant, or a lawyer, and nothing it shows you is advice or a recommendation. The numbers it produces come from figures you enter and from general assumptions that may not fit your situation. Talk to a qualified professional before making a decision about your job, your money, or starting a business.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this same address with a new date at the top. Anything that would change how data is handled would be described here before it took effect.
